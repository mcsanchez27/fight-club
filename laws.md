# Laws of the Court

Frameworks discovered in session. Rulings may cite these by name.

- **The Maki Law:** *skill compounds with stats; stats don't compound with anything.* The fighter who earned every win without margin becomes terrifying when handed margin. (Batman-with-Superman's-powers corollary.)
- **The Fuse vs. Battery test:** armies whose doctrine is "organize, then release fury" are detonations — they must finish on the first spend. Machines win every battle their geometry survives, and lose every battle it doesn't.
- **The Bugatti Doctrine:** elite one-dimensional forces are tactical masterpieces and operational liabilities. Total cost of ownership beats peak performance. *"Alone, an expensive death."*
- **Ramps must be priced:** Broly-class opponents convert contested time into tier-jumps. "Fast is the only speed at which anyone beats Broly at all."
- **Mechanics beat tiers in narrow windows** — Hakai, Time-Skip, soul-cutting — but sustained-will techniques fizzle under interrupt. (Manga Goku Hakai precedent.)
- **Top-tier fights are decided by character flaws** — the power levels long ago stopped being the constraint. Every ruling swung on pride, patience, or grief.
- **Logistics eat legends.** Continental-scale objectives defeat perfect armies; win conditions decide wars before armies do.
- **The Last Alliance theorem:** the best army in fantasy isn't best because it wins fights — the *genre of fight that beats armies* doesn't apply to it. To beat it, change the question.
- **Corpse-conversion math:** against the dead, your casualties are enemy reinforcements — preservation doctrine only; speed-bump strategies feed the tide.
- **Responsibility ≠ accountability:** self-imposed virtue is a king having a good afternoon. Name the mechanism.
- **The trap clause:** contingencies declared "from the beginning" are admissible, and the court has learned to check the floor before opening anything the King hands it.
